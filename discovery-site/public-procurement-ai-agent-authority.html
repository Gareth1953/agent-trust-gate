AI Agent Authority for Public Procurement

Public money should not be committed by AI on assumed authority.

Agent Trust Gate™ demonstrates a pre-action authority layer for consequential procurement events from requisition and supplier selection through purchase order, receipt, invoice approval and payment release.

The control question

Existing systems may record that an approval exists. ATG asks whether the approving person or agent was actually authorised at that moment for that exact supplier, value, account, department, jurisdiction, framework and action, and whether the executed action still matches what was approved.

Illustrative procurement actions

  • issue or amend a purchase order;
  • change supplier or quantity;
  • approve a contract variation;
  • record or dispute goods receipt;
  • approve an invoice;
  • release a payment or refuse it when order, receipt and invoice do not match.

Risk-based authority

A low-value repeat order can have a narrower automated path. Higher-value or unusual actions can require stronger evidence and Verified Human Authority. Strategic or highly sensitive procurement can require multiple authorities and stricter policy. If authority is missing, expired, exceeded or unverifiable, the action fails closed.

What ATG is not

ATG does not replace procurement law, delegated-authority frameworks, ERP or e-procurement systems, finance controls, contracting officers or government approval processes. The public implementation is a local deterministic demonstrator with synthetic data and no real procurement, payment, settlement or external action capability.
