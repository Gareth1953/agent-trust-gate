# Enterprise Positioning Validation Pack

**Mission:** P3-M156A<br>
**Authority:** Gareth Price<br>
**Status:** Local validation pack only; synthetic, deterministic, reviewer-ready and non-production.

## Approved positioning

**Category:** Enterprise AI Action Trust and Governance — initially delivered as a local exact-action evaluation.

**Headline:** “Verify authority before a consequential AI-agent action.”

**Buyer explanation:** “Agent Trust Gate™ lets an enterprise test whether an agent’s proposed action has the mandate, approvals, limits and evidence the business requires before anything irreversible happens.”

**Supporting explanation:** “Agent Trust Gate™ demonstrates how agent identity, accountable principal, delegation, verified human authority and exact-action evidence can produce a GatePass—or a refusal—before action, with separate execution evidence afterwards.”

The controlling principles remain:

> Verify Before Settlement.

> No mandate. No evidence. No signed GatePass. No settlement.

> Agent Trust Gate™ exists to make digital trust verifiable before action, observable during execution and accountable afterwards.

## Maturity boundary

ATG is a local-first, synthetic, protocol-agnostic and deterministic demonstrator prepared for reviewer evaluation. It is not connected to a company directory, ERP, bank or payment system and processes no customer, supplier or employee data. It does not provide certification, a fraud-prevention guarantee, production assurance or a complete AI-transformation platform.

The pack keeps five claims separate:

1. **Identity verification** checks fixture evidence about the declared agent or human identity.
2. **Authority verification** evaluates current fixture delegation, role, limits and separation of duties.
3. **Business-policy evaluation** applies the configured supplier-change rules.
4. **GatePass decision** allows only one exact, fresh, one-use master-data proposal or refuses it.
5. **Execution evidence** is a separate receipt state; a GatePass never proves that an ERP change occurred.

Cryptographic fixture evidence can support integrity, binding and signer-related evidence within the configured local model. It does not prove honesty, truth, legality, fraud absence, bank-information correctness, competence, compliance, payment validity or general agent safety.

## Vertical-first strategy

**Commercial growth principle:** “Start with one consequential workflow. Prove the control. Test whether the same trust model can govern a second workflow without rebuilding the core.”

**Design principle:** “Land vertically through one consequential action. Test whether the same trust model can be reused horizontally across a second workflow.”

Horizontal reuse is a hypothesis to test after the first workflow. It has not been commercially proven. The initial evidence should show whether the identity, principal, delegation, exact-action, human-authority, decision and receipt primitives can be mapped to a second workflow without claiming business-wide deployment.

## Selected supplier-change wedge

The fictional wedge is a supplier bank-detail change before an ERP or payment-master-data update. The reference fixture concerns `Northbridge Office Systems Ltd`, supplier `SUP-10482`, changing an account ending from `1846` to `7319`. These are invented labels and four-digit endings, not real supplier or bank data.

The control chain is:

```text
agent identity
  -> accountable principal
  -> delegated supplier-maintenance authority
  -> Agent Standing
  -> exact proposed bank-detail change
  -> independent-verification evidence
  -> authorised human approval
  -> separation of duties and dual approval
  -> freshness, expiry and replay state
  -> exact-action digest
  -> GatePass or refusal
  -> separate simulated execution evidence
```

A GatePass is limited to the exact proposed master-data change. It gives no authority for payment, invoice approval, contract acceptance, supplier creation, broader ERP access, unrelated or future changes, repeat use or settlement.

## Permitted claims

- ATG locally demonstrates exact-action evaluation for synthetic supplier-change scenarios.
- The evaluator distinguishes agent identity, accountable principal, delegation, human authority, business policy, GatePass decision and execution evidence.
- It deterministically refuses missing, stale, mismatched, replayed, revoked or out-of-scope fixture evidence.
- A one-use GatePass can be bound to one synthetic supplier master-data proposal.
- The trust model is designed to test reuse across additional workflows; reuse remains unproven.

## Validation questions

1. Can the reviewer explain the product as an exact-action trust evaluation rather than a broad transformation platform?
2. Can the reviewer identify the supplier bank-detail change as the first workflow?
3. Can the reviewer distinguish login from current, scoped authority for one action?
4. Are independent verification, separation of duties and dual approval visible and credible as configured policy checks?
5. Do the account, supplier, digest, expiry, replay, standing and authority refusals fail closed with usable reasons?
6. Is the difference between a GatePass decision and execution evidence unmistakable?
7. Are the maturity, data, integration, cryptographic and commercial limitations clear?
8. Could existing controls already satisfy the organisation, and does the assessment explicitly test that possibility?
9. Does exact-action evidence appear valuable enough to justify a paid assessment?
10. Is the next step clear without inviting real or sensitive data?
